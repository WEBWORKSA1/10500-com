Why 10,500 is not 一万五

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20266 min read

Ask a Mandarin learner to say 10,500 and many will answer 一万五. A Chinese shopkeeper hears 15,000. That one missing character, 零, is worth ¥4,500. Here's how the system works.

1. Chinese counts in 万, not thousands

English groups digits in threes (thousand, million, billion). Chinese groups them in fours: 万 (wàn, 10⁴) and 亿 (yì, 10⁸). Regroup 10,500 the Chinese way and you get 1,0500: one 万, then 0500.

10,500 → 1 | 0500 → 一万 + 零 + 五百 = 一万零五百

2. The zero rule (零)

When one or more places in the middle are empty, say 零 once. The thousands place in 0500 is empty, so 零 appears. Trailing zeros are never spoken.

10,500一万零五百thousands place empty → 零

10,005一万零五three empty places → still one 零
15,000一万五千 (一万五)nothing empty in the middle; trailing unit may be dropped
105,000十万五千10|5000: the lower group starts right at the thousands place, so there's no gap and no 零
100,500十万零五百10|0500: the thousands place is empty → 零

3. The colloquial shortcut that causes the trap

In speech, when a number ends with the unit directly below the last named one, that unit is dropped: 一万五千 → 一万五, 一千五百 → 一千五, 三百五十 → 三百五. That's why 一万五 must mean 15,000. For 10,500 there's a gap, so you need 零, and the shortcut can't apply.

A 3-step method for any number

  1. Regroup by four from the right: 123456789 → 1|2345|6789.
  2. Read each group with 千/百/十 and attach 亿 or 万: 一亿 | 二千三百四十五万 | 六千七百八十九.
  3. Insert 零 wherever a gap appears between non-zero digits (including at the start of a group).

Money, cheques & 大写

On invoices and cheques, 10,500 yuan is written 壹万零伍佰元整. Capital numerals stop anyone turning 一 into 二 or 三 with a stroke of the pen. The same zero rule applies.
