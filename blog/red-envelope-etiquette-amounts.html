Red envelope etiquette & amounts by occasion

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20267 min read

A red envelope (红包 hóngbāo in Mandarin, 利是 lai see in Cantonese) carries a wish as well as money. Get the amount, the numbers and the manners right and you'll make a good impression.

🧧 Skip ahead: calculate your amount

The five golden rules

  1. Red for joy, white for grief. Never use red at a funeral.
  2. Even and lucky amounts for celebrations: 6, 8 and 9 are good, and 88, 168, 888 and 1314 are popular.
  3. No 4s: not 4, 40, 400 or 1,400.
  4. New, crisp notes, with no coins.
  5. Both hands to give and receive, and don't open it in front of the giver.

Weddings

The classic guideline is to cover your seat at the banquet, then scale up for closeness. Typical starting points:

Lunar New Year

Married adults give to children and unmarried younger relatives, and employers give to staff. Amounts in Hong Kong tend to be modest (HK$20–HK$100 for acquaintances' children), while mainland families often give more. Say 恭喜发财 (gōng xǐ fā cái) or 新年快乐.

Birthdays, babies & graduations

  • Elder's milestone birthday (60, 70, 80): generous, with 9s for longevity.
  • Baby's full month (满月): an even, lucky amount. Gold jewellery is a traditional alternative.
  • Graduation / exams: 6s wish smooth progress.

Funerals

Condolence money (帛金 / 白金) goes in a white envelope. Many communities give odd amounts (e.g., 101, 201, 501) and expect a small token in return, sometimes a coin and a sweet, meant to be used or eaten before you get home.

Digital red packets

On WeChat and Alipay, digital hongbao are everywhere, including “grab” packets in group chats. The same number rules apply, and romantic amounts like ¥520 and ¥1,314 peak on May 20 and Qixi.
