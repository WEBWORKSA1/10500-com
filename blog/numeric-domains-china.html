Why China loves numeric domains

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20266 min read

In most countries, top websites use words. In China, many household names are numbers. Here's why, and what that means for anyone building a brand around a number.

Famous examples

DomainBrandWhy it works
163.comNetEase (网易)A short, early number that became a major email and portal brand
58.com58 Tongcheng (58同城)Classifieds. “58” is short, and 8 suggests prosperity
360.cnQihoo 360“360 degrees” of protection, understood in any language
4399.com4399 gamesA memorable pattern, despite the 4
12306.cnChina Railway ticketsMirrors the railway hotline number
hao123.comBaidu's link directory好 (good) + 123 (easy)

Why numbers beat words in China

  1. Typing: for many users, Latin-letter spellings of Chinese words are ambiguous (does 网易 become wangyi, wangyee or netease?), while digits are universal.
  2. Memorability: numbers read as phrases, like 58 (我发, “I prosper”, in some readings) or 51 (我要, “I want”).
  3. Symbolism: a lucky number adds positive meaning at no cost.
  4. Offline-to-online: numbers match hotline numbers, prices and ads.

What makes a numeric domain valuable

  • Length: shorter is scarcer. All two- and three-digit .coms were registered long ago.
  • Digits: 8, 6 and 9 add value, 4 tends to subtract, and 0 and 1 are neutral.
  • Pattern: repeats (888), sequences (123) and phrases (168, 518, 520).
  • Meaning: a real-world tie, like a date, a price, an area code, or a figure like 10500 (≈ the average monthly wage).
  • Extension: .com is still the global standard, and .cn is strong domestically.
