China's average salary in 2025: what ¥10,500 a month really means

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20266 min read

Here's a coincidence that makes our domain name useful: ¥10,500 is almost exactly the average monthly wage of an employee in China's urban non-private sector. The official numbers follow.

The official data

2025¥129,441 (+4.3%)¥10,787¥71,590 (+3.0%)¥5,966

The 2024 and 2025 monthly equivalents sit on either side of ¥10,500. In 2025, pay in non-private units (state agencies, public institutions, SOEs and foreign-invested firms) was about 81% higher than in private units.

From gross to take-home

A worker earning ¥10,500 gross typically pays about 10.5% in social insurance (pension 8%, medical 2%, unemployment 0.5%) plus 5–12% into the housing fund, before income tax on the rest above ¥5,000. With a 7% housing fund and no special deductions, that works out to roughly ¥8,500–¥8,600 net per month. Try your own numbers in the China Salary Calculator.

Averages hide wide gaps

  • Sector: IT, finance and scientific research sit well above the average, while hospitality and agriculture sit well below.
  • Region: Beijing, Shanghai and Shenzhen far exceed inland provinces.
  • Mean vs median: high earners pull the mean up, so a typical worker earns less than the average.

What it means for businesses

  • Budgeting a hire: the employer's social-insurance and housing-fund share adds roughly 30–40% on top of gross salary, depending on city.
  • Pricing to consumers: a ¥10,500 month anchors what “premium” means to the urban middle class.
  • Relocating staff: benchmark by city and sector, not the national mean.
