Number etiquette for doing business in China

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20267 min read

You don't have to believe in lucky numbers, but your customers, partners and staff may notice them. Here's a practical checklist.

✅ Do

  • End prices in 8 (¥88, ¥168, ¥1,688, ¥10,888) for premium products aimed at Chinese-speaking buyers.
  • Give gifts in pairs: two bottles of wine, two boxes of tea.
  • Pick launch and signing dates with 6, 8 or 9 where you can. Big deals are often scheduled around Lunar New Year too.
  • Write contract amounts in 大写 as well as figures: 壹万零伍佰元整. Use our converter.
  • Give staff red envelopes at Lunar New Year, with consistent amounts within each level.

❌ Don't

  • Don't price at 4s (¥44, ¥1,400) or give anything in a set of four.
  • Don't give clocks (送钟 sounds like attending a funeral), umbrellas (伞 ~ 散, separation), or sharp objects (cutting ties).
  • Don't schedule a big launch in the 7th lunar month (Ghost Month) if your audience observes it.
  • Don't assign floor-4 or room-14 offices to Chinese VIP visitors if there's an alternative.
  • Don't give ¥250, which is slang for “idiot”.

Phone numbers & plates for your business

A memorable, lucky company number (e.g., ending 8888 or 1688) is a real marketing asset in Chinese-speaking markets. Check candidates with the Lucky Number Analyzer, or ask our premium number concierge to source one.

Regional differences

  • Cantonese regions (Guangdong, Hong Kong, Macau): 3 (生, life) and 28 (easy prosperity) are especially valued.
  • Taiwan: wedding amounts like 1,200 / 1,600 / 3,600 / 6,600 are common.
  • Younger urban consumers treat much of this playfully, but they still read 520 as “I love you”.
