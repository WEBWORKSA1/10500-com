The complete guide to Chinese lucky numbers

By 10500 EditorialUpdated Sept 28, 20269 min read

In Chinese, numbers are heard as words. A phone number can sound like a blessing or a curse, and people pay for that difference. Here's the system, digit by digit.

Why numbers carry meaning

Mandarin has a small set of syllables, so many words share a sound. Numbers borrow the meaning of the words they sound like. This is homophony (谐音, xiéyīn), the same mechanism behind puns in New Year decorations: 鱼 (fish) sounds like 余 (surplus), so fish is served for abundance.

Famous combinations

  • 168 一路发: prosper all the way. Shop names, prices, phone numbers.
  • 518 我要发: “I will prosper.”
  • 888 / 8888: triple and quadruple prosperity.
  • 666: smooth, and online, “awesome”.
  • 1314 一生一世: forever. The rare positive 4.
  • 14, 514, 74: 要死, 我要死, 气死. Avoid them.
  • 250 二百五: “idiot”. Never give ¥250.

What luck is worth

Superstition has a price. The Chengdu phone number 8888-8888 sold for ¥2.33 million in August 2003 (Guinness World Records). In 2016 the Hong Kong licence plate “28” (Cantonese: easy prosperity) sold for HK$18.1 million, about US$2.3 million, according to Bloomberg, which also reported plates 18 and 9 fetching roughly US$2.1M and US$1.7M. The Beijing Olympics opened at 8:08 pm on 8/8/2008.

The other side shows up too. Buildings skip 4th, 14th and 24th floors, and some developers relabel them. Addresses heavy in 4s can take longer to sell to buyers who care.

Internet number slang

Chinese netizens type numbers as shorthand: 520 (我爱你, I love you), 88 (bye-bye), 233 (lol), 555 (crying), 886 (bye then). May 20 (5/20) has become an unofficial Valentine's Day, when couples send ¥520 digital red packets.

Using this in real life

  1. Pricing for Chinese-speaking buyers: end on 8 and avoid 4. Test prices with the analyzer.
  2. Gifts: give in pairs, not fours, and use lucky amounts. See the red envelope calculator.
  3. Dates: weddings and launches cluster on 6s, 8s, 9s and 520/1314 dates.
  4. Respect, not fear: many younger Chinese treat this playfully, but it's polite to avoid giving offence with a 4.
