China Business Guide 商务指南

Numbers are the most visible part of Chinese business culture. Here is what they signal, plus the etiquette, wage data and sourcing checklist you need before your first deal.

1. Numbers in pricing, gifts & dates

  • Pricing: endings in 8 (¥188, ¥1,688, ¥10,888) signal prosperity. Endings in 4 can hurt conversion with Chinese-speaking buyers.
  • Gifts: give in pairs (好事成双), never in fours, and never give clocks (送钟 sounds like attending a funeral).
  • Dates: launches and signings often fall on dates with 6, 8 or 9. The 7th lunar month (Ghost Month) is avoided for big decisions in many communities.
  • Addresses & floors: offices on floors 4, 14 or 24 can be harder to lease to Chinese tenants.

2. Meeting & banquet etiquette

  • Offer and receive business cards with both hands, Chinese side up, and read them before putting them away.
  • Address people by title and surname (e.g., 王总, “President Wang”).
  • At banquets the host sits facing the door. Wait to be seated, and toast with your glass lower than a senior person's.
  • Relationships (关系) come before contracts. Expect several meetings before a deal closes.

4. Supplier sourcing checklist

  1. Verify the business licence (营业执照) and that its scope covers your product.
  2. Check whether it is a real factory or a trading company. Both can work, but know which.
  3. Request certifications relevant to your market (CE, FCC, FDA, ISO 9001…).
  4. Order pre-production samples, and keep a signed “golden sample”.
  5. Use a written contract that sets out specs, tolerances, packaging, Incoterms and penalties.
  6. Pay a deposit of 30% or less, with the balance after a pre-shipment inspection.
  7. Protect IP: register trademarks in China early (it is a first-to-file system), and use NNN agreements.

5. Market-entry basics

Common routes are a representative office (liaison only), a WFOE (wholly foreign-owned enterprise, which can invoice and hire), a joint venture, or cross-border e-commerce without a local entity. Each has different capital, tax and licensing requirements, so get local advice.
