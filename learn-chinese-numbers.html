Level 2 · 11–99

Chinese numbers are logical: 11 = 十一 (ten-one), 20 = 二十 (two-ten), 99 = 九十九 (nine-ten-nine). If you know 0–10, you already know 0–99.

Level 3 · Hundreds, thousands & the zero rule

百 (bǎi) = 100 and 千 (qiān) = 1,000. When a place in the middle is empty, say 零 once: 105 = 一百零五 and 1,005 = 一千零五. Trailing zeros are silent: 1,500 = 一千五百, which is often shortened to 一千五.

Level 4 · 万 (10,000) and 亿 (100,000,000)

This is where English speakers get stuck. Chinese groups digits in fours: 1,0500 rather than 10,500.

10,000一万yī wàn
10,500一万零五百yī wàn líng wǔ bǎi
15,000一万五(千)yī wàn wǔ (qiān)
100,000十万shí wàn
1,000,000一百万yī bǎi wàn
100,000,000一亿yī yì
Trap: 一万五 = 15,000. For 10,500 you must say 一万零五百. Why?

Level 5 · Phone numbers, money & dates

  • Phones: read digit by digit, with 1 as yāo: 138 = 幺三八
  • Money: ¥10,500 = 一万零五百块 (spoken) or 元 (written).
  • Dates: year digit by digit + 年, then month + 月, day + 日/号: 2026年8月8日 = 二零二六年八月八日.
  • 两 vs 二: 两个 (two items) and 两万 (20,000), but 十二 (12).
